The Contestability Index

How contestable are digital platform markets? Sixteen pro-contestability obligation categories, scored on the law as written across nine jurisdictions, and a de facto pilot measuring what actually changed in two European markets after the Digital Markets Act.

⚠

Law on the books is not the same as a contestable market

The European Union mandates every one of the sixteen obligation categories in full: its de jure score is the maximum the index can award. Yet in the two European markets where we measured observed conditions, contestability after the Digital Markets Act remains far below what the law requires. The distance between the two bars is the finding.

De jure = the market-level legal framework in the European Union, June 2026. De facto = observed market conditions for that gatekeeper–market pair, July 2026. The paper gives four ways of relating the two layers; the table under the bars reports all four, at the December 2023 baseline and at the latest observation.

Read the scope before the scores. The de facto layer covers three gatekeeper–market pairs in two European Union markets, observed at two points in time. It is not a nine-jurisdiction measure, and nothing on this page should be compared directly with another jurisdiction's de jure score.
