The Contestability Index

How contestable are digital platform markets? Fifteen pro-contestability obligation categories, scored on the law as written across nine jurisdictions, and a de facto pilot measuring what actually changed in two European markets after the Digital Markets Act.

⚠

Law on the books is not the same as a contestable market

The European Union mandates every one of the fifteen obligation categories in full: its de jure score is the maximum the index can award. Yet in the two European markets where we measured observed conditions, contestability after the Digital Markets Act remains far below what the law requires. The distance between the two bars is the finding.

De jure = the enacted legal framework for the European Union. De facto = observed market conditions for that core platform service, post-DMA. The paper offers four ways of relating the two layers and does not privilege one; this page shows the gap because it keeps both components visible. The methodology page lists all four.
